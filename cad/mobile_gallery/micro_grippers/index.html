What makes fine motion work?

ElementWhat to inspect / borrowWhat can defeat it
Jaw-tip geometryNarrow, aligned contact faces give access and define where force lands. A needle driver grips a needle; a tissue grasper has a different contact task.Tiny tips increase local pressure and fragility. They do not measure placement accuracy.
Hinge + remote actuationSmall distal pivots put articulation near the work while larger motors stay proximal. EndoWrist uses internal cables. A push/pull rod is an alternative for a simpler single-axis tool.Cable stretch/slack, pivot clearance, friction, rod buckling and transmission hysteresis all produce lost motion.
Stiffness + preloadShort force paths and supported pivots reduce deflection. A return spring can keep a single cable taut; opposed tendons can preload both directions.Preload consumes force and raises bearing load. More tension is not automatically more accuracy.
Motion scalingA remote master can command smaller slave motion; tremor filtering is a separate control function. Mechanical reduction can trade stroke/speed for finer jaw rotation.A directly held tool still follows hand translation and tremor. Jaw reduction cannot stabilize its whole pose.

Mechanism discussion is engineering interpretation, not a reconstruction of NanoWrist internals. Cable effects are documented in Hwang et al., cable-driven dVRK calibration; motion scaling is described by Intuitive and MMI above.

A surgical grasper versus a gripper holding an instrument

In the first, the tool's own jaws contact the target. In the second, our jaws clamp a separate instrument's handle or shaft; that instrument has its own tip, actuation and alignment. Attaching a tweezer to our gripper would add an interface and would not reproduce a wristed surgical instrument.

Motor attachment check · current design unchanged

Your four-screw recollection fits the SO-101 horn

The SO-101 guide uses four M3 screws into the rotating STS3215 horn, plus a separate center retaining screw. It also uses four smaller M2 screws to mount the gripper motor case. Base 25 uses a different motor: the HLS3606M-C001 from Aero Hand. Its smaller metal plate has eight holes, of which our pinion uses two. These are different interfaces; no motor swap or screw-pattern transplant is proposed.

Actual bolt count: 9 before, 9 after this study

Current Base 25 has 2 plate screws + 1 center retaining screw + 4 case screws + 2 jaw pivot bolts = 9 screws/bolts. Two pivot nuts and two washers are counted separately. With four printed solids, the motor and bought plate, that is 19 physical parts. This study removes zero bolts and adds no adapters. The cable/spring concept would add hardware; it is not a demonstrated part-count reduction.

A later simplification target is 9 → 7 screws by replacing four case-post screws with two diagonally opposed screws only if an integral keyed cradle takes servo reaction torque, locates the case on hard shoulders and positively captures lift. Retain both plate screws, center screw and both pivot bolts. Removing the center screw would lose spline axial capture; relying on friction or a clip alone is not the proposed solution.

That cradle must still expose both driver paths and permit fitting the spline plate/pinion before closing the mount. It needs a preload/loosening strategy: captured prevailing-torque nuts where accessible, verified resin-compatible self-tapping retention elsewhere, and only supplier-approved thread retention on metal threads. Prove reversal, vibration, pull-out and creep resistance under the intended load before deleting fasteners. No such reduction is validated here, so it gets no new part number yet.

Exploratory proposal · no printable files

One cable, one return spring, the same original tips

Carry the motor-interface check above into the later experiment; keep the complete center retention and case mounting unless the keyed-cradle reduction is proved. For a later branch from Base 25 / Gripper 29: keep the complete finger contact band, lower counter-rotating gear pair and low pivot sleeves. Replace the upper gear drive with an integral drum on one jaw and a small crank on the bought servo plate. A short cable pulls the drum; one return spring opens the jaws and maintains tension. The lower gear pair still synchronizes them. This avoids a second motor, idler or instrument adapter.

Retain the HLS3606M-C001 centered above the finger plane, its four actual case-ear screws, bought spline plate, opposed plate screws and center retention. No assumed STS3215 hole pattern or printed spline. Cable and spring must stay overhead and outside the original contact approach. Four printed solids could remain, but cable, terminations and spring add physical parts and assembly work.

Illustrative leverage, not an accuracy claim

For an ideal taut line near a tangent, jaw angle ≈ (crank radius / drum radius) × servo angle. An assumed 6 mm crank and 18 mm drum would give about 0.33° jaw motion per servo degree locally, versus 28/18 ≈ 1.56 for the current upper gears. That is about 4.7× less angular motion for the same motor increment, with a nominal 90° servo travel for a 30° jaw stroke. A finite crank sweep is nonlinear: those values are not a full-stroke design or valid servo commands. Clearance may force different radii or a different layout.

The spring consumes grip torque, cable routing may need an extra guide, and the remaining lower gears still have backlash. This proposal is not yet known to fit, back-drive comfortably, or maintain tip contact under load.

Next design pass

First print and measure the preserved candidate. For the experiment, solve cable tangent geometry and full stroke before making new CAD. Check bought-plate screw access, swept cable/spring clearance, ear mounting and original-tip contact. Measure tip displacement through reversals under a microscope or calibrated camera, with and without a known load; record repeatability, hysteresis, stiffness, minimum controllable movement and opening force. Motor command resolution and a small tip are not accuracy measurements.

Any later CAD must take the newest part versions at that time and bump only changed parts. This study stays outside the version catalogue so it cannot replace the current print choice.
